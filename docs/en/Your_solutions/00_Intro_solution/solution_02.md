# Systems of Equations: A Step-by-Step Presentation

## Step 1: The Setup (Introduction)
* **What to Write:** 1) $2x + 3y = 12$
    2) $x - y = 1$
* **What to Say:** "Professor, classmates, hello. Today we are solving a system of linear equations. Our goal is to find the specific values for $x$ and $y$ that make both of these equations true at the exact same time. To do this clearly and efficiently, we will use the **Substitution Method**."

---

## Step 2: The Strategy (Isolating a Variable)
* **What to Write:** $x - y = 1 \implies x = y + 1$
* **What to Say:** "We always look for the path of least resistance. The second equation is much simpler, so let's use it to isolate $x$. By moving $-y$ to the other side of the equals sign, we discover a key piece of information: $x$ is exactly equal to $y + 1$."

---

## Step 3: The Execution (Substitution)
* **What to Write:** $2(y + 1) + 3y = 12$
    $2y + 2 + 3y = 12$
    $5y + 2 = 12 \implies 5y = 10 \implies \mathbf{y = 2}$
* **What to Say:** "Now we put that information to work. In our first equation, we replace the $x$ variable with $(y + 1)$. This gives us a new equation with only one unknown variable. We distribute the 2, combine our like terms to get $5y + 2 = 12$, and solve to find that $y$ is exactly equal to 2."

---

## Step 4: Finding the Second Variable
* **What to Write:** $x = y + 1$
    $x = 2 + 1 \implies \mathbf{x = 3}$
* **What to Say:** "Since we now know $y$ is 2, finding $x$ is incredibly straightforward. We plug our $y$ value back into our isolated equation from Step 2. Two plus one gives us our $x$ value, which is 3."

---

## Step 5: The Big Picture (Verification)
* **What to Write:** **Solution: (3, 2)**
    Check: $2(3) + 3(2) = 6 + 6 = 12$ *(Correct)*
* **What to Say:** "Our final solution is $x = 3$ and $y = 2$. As a final, professional check, if we plug these numbers back into our original, more complex equation, $6 + 6$ indeed equals 12. The system is perfectly balanced.
